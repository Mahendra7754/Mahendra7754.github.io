01 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Indoor positioning without GPS.

Small quadrotors require reliable position and attitude estimates to support closed-loop flight. Indoors, satellite navigation is unavailable. This project investigates an external stereo-vision system that observes infrared markers mounted on a mini quadrotor, reconstructs their 3D locations and estimates the vehicle's pose.

Scope completed: vision-based localization, marker-based orientation estimation, linear position Kalman filtering and experimental validation. Closed-loop position and attitude control remain part of the continuing project.

03 / HARDWARE DEVELOPMENT

From a single marker to a rigid three-LED assembly.

Hardware: two InnoMaker OV9281 monochrome global-shutter cameras with infrared band-pass filters; mini quadrotor equipped with custom LED marker assembly.

Position Kalman filter

Detected image points are corrected using the calibrated camera parameters and triangulated using the relative camera pose. The reconstructed three-point geometry defines a marker coordinate frame for yaw, pitch and roll estimation. A constant-velocity linear Kalman filter estimates position and velocity and can predict through temporary measurement loss.

Technical decision: point undistortion rather than full-frame rectification

During real-time tracking, only the detected LED coordinates were undistorted. The full images were not rectified. This avoided resampling complete frames and preserved the useful observed image area for the chosen camera arrangement.

Technical decision: scalene three-LED geometry

Three non-collinear markers enable construction of the orientation axes. Unequal triangle side lengths help distinguish the LED identities, although mislabeling can still produce attitude outliers.

05 / CALIBRATION

Calibrating the cameras before 3D reconstruction.

Intrinsic calibration estimates focal lengths, principal points and distortion coefficients. Stereo calibration determines the cameras' relative rotation and translation, enabling reconstruction of 3D marker locations from corresponding normalized image points.

07 / ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

What the experiments revealed.

Depth sensitivity

Stereo depth becomes increasingly sensitive to image-coordinate errors at longer working distances. The depth comparison also reveals positions with reduced repeatability.

Correspondence and labeling

Incorrect identification of the three LED points can produce large orientation errors. Marker geometry improves identification but does not eliminate outliers.

Coordinate-frame consistency

Calibration, triangulation and room-frame transformation must remain geometrically consistent. Scale and room-registration validation remain important considerations.

Real-time constraints

Acquisition, detection, matching, triangulation and filtering were balanced to achieve approximately 19 Hz processing.
