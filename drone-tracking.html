01. The engineering problem

Indoor drone tracking requires position and orientation information without relying on satellite navigation. The goal of this thesis was to develop and experimentally evaluate an external vision system capable of estimating the pose of a mini quadrotor in a defined indoor workspace.

02. System architecture

Two InnoMaker OV9281 monochrome global-shutter cameras observe infrared LEDs mounted in a rigid three-marker arrangement on the drone. Infrared band-pass filters help isolate the markers from the scene. The software detects bright image blobs, establishes stereo correspondence, undistorts the detected coordinates and reconstructs the marker positions in 3D.

The three reconstructed markers are used to estimate orientation. A constant-velocity linear Kalman filter smooths position measurements and predicts the state when measurements are temporarily unavailable.

Engineering detail: Only detected marker coordinates were undistorted for triangulation. The full image frames were not rectified during real-time tracking.

03. Calibration & reconstruction

Intrinsic calibration provides camera parameters and distortion coefficients. Stereo calibration estimates the relative rotation and translation between cameras. Undistorted, normalized point coordinates are triangulated using projection matrices P₁ = [I | 0] and P₂ = [R | T]. Reconstructed coordinates are subsequently transformed into the room coordinate frame.

04. Orientation estimation

A scalene triangular arrangement of three LEDs enables identification of the marker points and construction of the drone's orientation axes. The orientation is expressed through yaw, pitch and roll. The approach was evaluated for repeatability and angular error.

Three rectangular trajectory experiments produced edge RMSE values of 1.260 cm, 0.736 cm and 1.578 cm. These figures describe deviation from the reference rectangle edges, not general-purpose absolute 3D localization accuracy. The yaw experiment produced a mean absolute error of 7.96°, with occasional large errors associated with marker identification.

06. Challenges & lessons

  • Depth sensitivity: Stereo reconstruction becomes more sensitive to image-coordinate errors as working distance increases.
  • Marker correspondence: Incorrect LED labeling can cause substantial orientation outliers.
  • Coordinate frames: Camera-frame measurements must be consistently related to the room reference frame.
  • Performance trade-offs: The system balances image acquisition, detection, triangulation and filtering within the processing loop.

07. Continuation: Fly Crazy Fly

Fly Crazy Fly continues this work toward a more complete indoor autonomous drone system. The next proposed steps are integrating onboard IMU data with external vision measurements, developing and evaluating an EKF-based estimator, introducing ROS2 and ultimately investigating closed-loop flight control.

Development status: IMU–vision fusion, ROS2 integration and autonomous flight using the external tracking system have not yet been implemented. They are future objectives, not thesis results.
